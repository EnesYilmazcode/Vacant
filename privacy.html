Privacy.

Vacant is a free student project. This page says what it knows about you, which is very little, and it does not round any of it in the flattering direction.

Where you are stays on your phone. Vacant asks your browser for your location so it can sort rooms by how far away they are. That sorting happens on your phone, in the page, with the numbers your browser hands it. Your coordinates are never sent anywhere, because there is no Vacant server to send them to.

How that is true, and not just a promise

Vacant has no backend. The whole class schedule is a few static files on GitHub Pages that your phone downloads when you open the page. There is no login, no account, no database, and no request that could carry a coordinate even if somebody wanted it to.

Opening it does still need a connection. There is no offline cache yet, so a stairwell with no signal gets you the browser's error page rather than a room. That is worth saying plainly because the reason to have no backend is often given as "it works offline", and today it does not.

Your position is read once per search, held in a variable, and gone when you close the tab. The app asks for it once and does not keep watching. It is never written to storage, never put in a URL, and never attached to anything the app caches. You can check: every line of the app is at github.com/EnesYilmazcode/Vacant, and there are only four JavaScript files.

The app does store exactly one thing on your phone, and it is not about you: a key called vacant:duration holding how long you last said you needed a room, so it does not have to ask twice. Clearing your browser data removes it. That is the complete list.

What is known anyway

Two things, and saying "we collect nothing" would make both of them a lie.

  • GitHub sees an IP address. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub's servers see the address your phone connects from when it downloads the page and the schedule file. Vacant has no access to those logs and cannot connect them to a location or to each other.
  • Your browser remembers the location permission. That grant lives in your browser's settings, not in Vacant. You can revoke it at any time and the app will not notice, because it never stored anything to forget.

What is never done

  • No accounts, no sign-in, and no Ohio State credentials. Ever. Vacant will never ask you for a BuckID or a password, and any page that does is not this one.
  • No advertising, no sponsorship, no affiliate links, no paid tier and no donations. This is not a business and it is not allowed to become one.
  • No analytics, no tracking pixels, and no third-party scripts of any kind. The page loads nothing from any other company: no fonts, no map tiles, no SDKs.
  • Nothing is shared with Ohio State, or with anyone else. There is nothing to share.

If you say no to location

The app still works, and it tells you what it did. It falls back to the middle of the Oval and puts a line above the list saying so: Location is off, showing from the Oval. You get the same campus-wide room list either way. There is no building picker and you are never asked to choose one.

The walk times are the part to watch. They are still shown, and they are measured from the Oval, not from you. If you are at the Wexner Center or out on West Campus, every minute figure on the screen is the walk from the middle of campus, which can be wrong in either direction: too long for a room you are standing next to, too short for one across the river. That banner is the only warning you get, so read it as "these minutes are not about you". Turning location on is what makes them yours.

What Vacant does not know about the rooms

Not a privacy point, but it belongs on the page a suspicious person opens. Vacant knows what the Registrar publishes and nothing more. It knows no class is scheduled in a room. It does not know whether a club booked it, whether the building's outer door is locked, or whether somebody is already sitting in there. When it does not know, it says so instead of guessing. Read docs/DATA.md for exactly what it fetches and how often.

If room reports ever ship

They have not. There is no backend today and no report button anywhere in the app. This paragraph is written in advance so the design is settled before there is a deadline pushing against it, and it goes live unchanged on the day the feature does.

When you report a room. Tapping "it was open" or "it was locked" would send the room and the half-hour block, and nothing else. Not your location, not the exact time, not who you are. We would add one to a counter. There would be no row anywhere recording that a particular person reported anything, because there would be no per-report rows at all. To stop one person voting a thousand times we would keep a scrambled, one-way number worked out from your connection for about a month, in a separate list that has no room in it, no building in it, and no way to be joined back to any room. A room's "usually open" label would only appear once at least three different people had reported it, so a single report is never visible to anyone.

The full design, including the parts that were rejected and why, is in docs/design/reports.md.

Is any of this legally required?

No, and that is worth saying out loud. Ohio has no consumer privacy law on the books. Nothing here touches a student record, so nothing here touches FERPA. This page exists because you should be able to check what an app on your phone is doing, not because a statute made someone write it.
